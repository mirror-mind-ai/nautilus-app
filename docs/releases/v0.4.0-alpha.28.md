# v0.4.0-alpha.28 - An update that says it will come back, and two fixes from Rodrigo

**Date:** 2026-11-26

## Highlights

- **An update says the app will come back.** When you click Update, the chip and Settings › Updates now say
  that Nautilus will close and reopen by itself in a few minutes, and that there is no need to open it
  again meanwhile. On Windows the app closes at once and the installer takes seconds to show; in that gap
  the app was opened again by hand (CR295).
- **A link inside bold or italic text opens.** A model that wrote a link in bold showed the brackets and
  the path as plain text; now the label is a link, in messages, in a copied message and in the document
  preview (CR297, PR #33 by Rodrigo).
- **A Settings checkbox label reads in the light themes.** "Prompt cache notices" and the two checkboxes of
  Diagnostics were pale on white in Daylight, Mist and Parchment, at about 1.4:1 (CR296, PR #32 by Rodrigo).
- **Still Intel-only and unsigned on macOS;** the Windows bundle is unsigned.

## Where We Started

Alpha.27 was the first release a Windows install could be offered as an update, and the update worked: the
virtual machine's alpha.26 was offered alpha.27, installed it and kept its Journey, its conversation and its
provider (CR287). It also showed a gap. The updater exits the app as soon as the installer starts, the
installer takes a few seconds to show, and in that silence the app was opened again from its shortcut,
which looked like a second installation. Two contributions from Rodrigo had also been waiting: a link in
bold that could not be clicked, and a label that could not be read in the light themes.

## What Changed

- **The sentence before the close (CR295).** One component, `UpdateRestartNote`, renders the sentence beside
  the progress text of the chip's popover and of Settings › Updates, from the click on Update. It appears
  in no other state, and the progress texts are unchanged. It is true on macOS too, where the app also
  closes and relaunches.
- **Bold and italic read what they hold (CR297).** Their text goes through the same inline reader as the
  text around them, so a link, a code span or a path inside them is read; a bare path inside bold becomes a
  link once the native side confirms the file, and a copied message keeps a link in bold as its label and
  destination. The document preview does the same.
- **The ink of a Settings checkbox (CR296).** `.provider-check` takes the theme's text colour, as every other
  label does. In the dark themes it is a little lighter than before.

## What Does Not Change

The same Pi, the same Node, the same Core (`mirror-mind@0.44.2`), the same Claude Code install and the same
Conversations as alpha.27. Nothing outside the frontend changed: no Rust, no new native command, no schema
change and no migration, the home staying at `018_drop_hook_injected`. The stored settings have the same
shape.

## Conscious Exclusions

- **The sentence disappears with the window.** It can only be read while the update downloads, a few
  seconds on a fast connection; after the app closes only the installer's own window is left. A line inside
  the installer would need a customised installer template, which is not part of this release.
- **Not yet seen in the app by the Navigator before the release:** the three changes were built and tested,
  and each record says what to look at; the first look is after this release.
- **Two Daylight lines of the prompt cache notice** sit just under 4.5:1 (the clock and the receipt); they
  were left as they are.
- **A third contribution from Rodrigo** (pasting an image into the composer) is open and not part of this
  release.
- **No signing or notarization,** on macOS or Windows, and **no Apple Silicon build.**

## What We Learned

A silent gap invites a second click. The update worked, and what failed was telling a person what was
happening while it did; the fix is a sentence, and a record of why it is there.

## Next Horizon

The first look at these three changes in a running app, and the update from alpha.27 to this release on
Windows and macOS. Then the Windows pages of the site. And, when Apple answers, signed and notarized
distribution.
